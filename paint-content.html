Fine settings — the parts the masters move, one by one
Everything follows the volume. Rings tile edge to edge; the outer one is thin and they swell toward the middle, up to 67 % of the hull's half width at the core, with the spine ribbon at 61 % on top — so a corvette gets one or two rings and a capital ship six to eight. The outer ribbon's width starts at 47 % of its range on the smallest hull and loses 3 % per 500 of volume. Inner rings are drawn 1.75× fatter than their band so ragged strips still fill a big hull (the outer ring and the spine are left alone). Engines are 30 % of the hull's half width ×1.32, one per 280 of volume, banking up in rows when the stern is short; each nozzle glows. Greebles from the style's own set are scattered over the plating, ×1.6 the engine size, some straddling the outline to break it (more of those on a small hull); the turret mounts from the same sets are kept in stock for the hard points. Plating moves the outer ribbon, the core, the spine and the fatness together; Fittings moves engines and greebles together; the fine settings under the flap add on top. Greeble seed reshuffles the scatter and nothing else — a nudge left or right is usually enough. Wash lays two colours along the rings — the first at the outline, the second at the core, so it follows the plating and stays symmetric — on the brighter greys only (the deep shadows stay dark) and never on the engine cones. Lights takes the plating's brightest specks (the top few per cent), paints them again in a colour and blooms them softly. The ship: the same editor as the Fleet Generator's. Drag chips into the tray (or onto the ship) — weapons (a chip is two guns, a drone chip four drones), roles dropped on a weapon (sniper, capital, point defence, anti-shield, anti-armour) and traits (armour, shield, fast, fabricator); the same chip again piles up. Double-click a stack (or ▾) for its details: what each gun does a second to a shield, to armour and to the hull, and its damage / rate / reach dials. The reactor, the weapons computer and the star drive size themselves, and the shield always grows to cover the ship — more shields make it stronger. Price: 🔓 the price follows the chips (add one or turn a dial and it goes up); drag the price (🔒) and the ship is built to it, the chips becoming emphasis. Under it the ship's character sheet. Painted at its own size paints it at its volume (untick it, or drag the size, to try the look at any size). The ship is a ship code — paste one into the box and it opens here — and ☆ Save keeps it in my ships, which the Fleet Generator puts into fleets. Weapons: the guns of the design (orbital bombardment is never shown) land where the game's own rule puts them (pairs mirrored across the spine, column after column from the front: missiles, then guns, then beams; an odd gun alone on the spine; hangars along the belly behind them). Every hard point gets a turret mount from the style's own set — one shape per kind — sized like the engines × the gun's size (×4 = twice as wide), never on the engines; greebles keep off them. The turret on top is the style's own art where it has some (Shards, Leaf, Leaf II and Serpent so far — a beam turret, a gun and a missile pod each, the body scaled to the mount and turning on its pivot), else the battle view's icon: a drum rimmed in the weapon's colour with a pale barrel. Beams and guns swing to the pointer (touch the ship and drag); a launcher sits fixed facing forward, as in the game; a hangar is a bay with no art yet. The sliders are offsets from the locked values — find a setting you like and read it off the line under the picture.
