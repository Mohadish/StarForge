How it works
Everything follows the volume. Rings tile edge to edge; the outer one is thin and they swell toward the middle, so a corvette gets one or two rings and a capital ship six to eight. Engines are one per 280 of volume, banking up in rows when the stern is short; each nozzle glows. Greebles from the style's own set are scattered over the plating, some straddling the outline to break it. Plating moves the outer ribbon, the core, the spine and the fatness together; Fittings moves engines and greebles together; the fine settings add on top. Greeble seed reshuffles the scatter and nothing else. Wash lays two colours along the rings, the first at the outline and the second at the core. Lights repaints the plating's brightest specks in a colour and blooms them. The 3D is built from the 2D: the hull painting is its roof, and the greebles, the hard points and the engines sit where they are painted. The ship: drag chips into the tray (or onto the ship); the same chip again piles up. Tap ▾ on a stack for its details.
