Every ship is rolled at random — hull, guns and their dials, armour, shields, thrusters — then auto-fitted (reactor, star drive, weapons computer) and trimmed to the same price. The same seed gives the same ships and the same fights. Fleets: the price is a FLEET's, split between its ships evenly, as a flagship with escorts, in tiers (corvettes, destroyers, a capital) or at random — no ship under 150, up to 25 a fleet; with different sizes every fight is a fleet-1 shape against a fleet-2 shape (one big ship against three small ones, say). Each ship picks its own target, holds its range from it and keeps clear of its wingmen; a fleet has lost when its last ship is gone. The Compositions card below says which splits and compositions won, and what countered what. Generations above 1: four ships of each kind, every ship fights every other, then the best of each kind breeds the next crop (itself kept, plus three tweaked children) — that many times. Watch a fight

…
Painted: every fleet draws a plating style and a two-colour wash from its name (the same in every fight, never the same style as the other side) and its ships wear the painter's skin — rings, spine, greebles, engines, lights, the turret mounts under the hard points and the style's own turrets on them. The skin is painted once per design when its sheets arrive; until then the plain plate shows. Controls: tap a ship and the camera rides it, close enough to watch its turrets swing and fire; wheel zooms round the cursor, drag pans (both stay on top of the following camera), middle button or ⌖ goes back to the whole fight; space pauses. Zoomed out, ships become tactical symbols — triangle / diamond / pentagon / hexagon / block from the smallest to the biggest ship in this fight — each a vessel filled with its hull like fluid in its side's colour, turning red as it drains, a purple arc for the shield; zoom in and the ship itself fades back with its bars. In command mode, right-click (long-press on a phone) an enemy ship for Eliminate / Keep away, or an empty spot for the area orders and Retreat (asked twice). Up close, every gun sits on its hard point and turns onto what it shoots (a launcher sits fixed, firing forward). Shots are drawn live, anchored to where things are at that moment: a beam flashes from the barrel to the target and is gone in a third of a second; a shell runs down to where the target is now and bursts on it; a missile flies all the way in and bursts where it lands. A hit the shield takes flashes purple at the shield's edge, a hit on the hull bursts orange. The shield is a purple skin over the ship, solid at its rim and fading fast inward, dimmer as it drains. ×1 runs the fight at half pace; ×3 and ×8 scale from there.
Each ship holds the distance where most of its guns reach: closes when too far, backs off when too close, circles when about right; the faster ship wins that argument, and the big dashed circle is the field's edge, where a runner gets cornered. Ships are drawn to scale (a corvette is about 30 u long, a beam reaches 200 u, a missile 2000 u). Bars: purple shield, grey armour (the shell outside the hull; layers ring outward and get bulky fast), green hull — once the hull is being hit the whole ship slows and fires slower, and a fabricator aboard patches it up to a point. Purple skin: shield. Small dashed circle: the range a ship wants. Cyan lines = beams, orange = kinetic shells, red darts = missiles in flight (they corkscrew in and burst on the hull), pale yellow chevrons = drones, which fly until they die. Yellow sparks are missiles swatted by point defence, white sparks are hits on drones. A gun with the point-defence role shoots missiles and drones first and the ship only when nothing is coming in — so a swarm costs the enemy the damage he was putting on you. Missiles and drones run out unless a fabricator aboard builds more. A drone flies on a battery: every unit flown and every volley drains it, and it keeps enough to get home — so a carrier standing 450 u off gets one volley per drone, one standing 150 u off gets seven. A hollow chevron is a drone heading home; a fabricator with stock refurbishes it, otherwise it is spent.

Your own ship, from chips

One weapon chip is two guns (a drone chip, four drones). Pile the same chip on a stack for emphasis — ×2, ×3. Drop a role on a weapon stack to make it a sniper, a capital gun, point defence… Traits pile up too: two armour chips, twice the armour. The ship is built for the same price as the rest, so emphasis is paid for by the other parts.

…or in words

Name weapons (beam, gun, missile, drone) with a count and a role if you like — sniper, capital, point defence, anti-shield, anti-armour — and traits: armoured, shielded, fast, fabricator. It is built for the same price as the others and joins the pool below, picked and ready to fight. Start with a count — 20 x 2 beams pd + fast — for a fleet of that many ships sharing the price; otherwise it takes fleet 1's shape.
