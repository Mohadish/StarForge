Fine settings — the parts the masters move, one by one
Everything follows the volume. Rings tile edge to edge; the outer one is thin and they swell toward the middle, up to 67 % of the hull's half width at the core, with the spine ribbon at 61 % on top — so a corvette gets one or two rings and a capital ship six to eight. The outer ribbon's width starts at 47 % of its range on the smallest hull and loses 3 % per 500 of volume. Inner rings are drawn 1.75× fatter than their band so ragged strips still fill a big hull (the outer ring and the spine are left alone). Engines are 30 % of the hull's half width ×1.32, one per 280 of volume, banking up in rows when the stern is short; each nozzle glows. Greebles from the style's own set are scattered over the plating, ×1.6 the engine size, some straddling the outline to break it (more of those on a small hull); the turret mounts from the same sets are kept in stock for the hard points. Plating moves the outer ribbon, the core, the spine and the fatness together; Fittings moves engines and greebles together; the fine settings under the flap add on top. Greeble seed reshuffles the scatter and nothing else — a nudge left or right is usually enough. Wash lays two colours along the rings — the first at the outline, the second at the core, so it follows the plating and stays symmetric — on the brighter greys only (the deep shadows stay dark) and never on the engine cones. Lights takes the plating's brightest specks (the top few per cent), paints them again in a colour and blooms them softly. Weapons: four kinds count (orbital bombardment is never shown) — give each a count and a size, and the hard points land where the game's own rule puts them (pairs mirrored across the spine, column after column from the front: missiles, then guns, then beams; an odd gun alone on the spine; hangars along the belly behind them). Every hard point gets a turret mount from the style's own set — one shape per kind — sized like the engines × the gun's size (×4 = twice as wide), never on the engines; greebles keep off them. The turret on top is the style's own art where it has some (Shards, Leaf, Leaf II and Serpent so far — a beam turret, a gun and a missile pod each, the body scaled to the mount and turning on its pivot), else the battle view's icon: a drum rimmed in the weapon's colour with a pale barrel. Beams and guns swing to the pointer (touch the ship and drag); a launcher sits fixed facing forward, as in the game; a hangar is a bay with no art yet. The sliders are offsets from the locked values — find a setting you like and read it off the line under the picture.
